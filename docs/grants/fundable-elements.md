# Fundable Elements — AI for Teachers

## Innovation Story

AI for Teachers flips the dominant narrative in education AI. While the industry races to build AI tutors for students, teachers — the people who actually shape learning — are being left behind. Most teachers receive zero formal training on AI. Those who do get one-off workshops that teach button-clicking, not thinking.

Skippy is an AI tutor that teaches *teachers* how to think about AI. It's a 7-week personalized course where an AI tutor named Skippy guides each teacher through a scaffolded curriculum — from mental model building to prompt engineering to ethical integration — producing one concrete, classroom-ready artifact per week.

**What makes it novel:**
- **AI teaching about AI**: Uses the technology it's teaching about, creating a meta-learning loop
- **Personalized at every layer**: Skippy knows each teacher's grade level, subjects, constraints, and goals — and adapts every response accordingly
- **Artifact-first pedagogy**: Every session ends with something the teacher can use Monday morning (a prompt template, a lesson plan workflow, a feedback rubric, a personal AI policy)
- **Builds independence, not dependency**: Explicitly sends teachers to test their skills in ChatGPT, Gemini, and other tools. Teaches transferable skills, not tool-specific tricks
- **Learning science foundation**: Uses SOLO taxonomy for diagnostic assessment, scaffolded progression, and metacognitive reflection

## Impact Thesis

**The problem:** 3.7 million U.S. public school teachers need AI literacy to prepare students for an AI-transformed world. But current PD approaches (workshops, webinars, self-guided courses) are generic, passive, and disconnected from teachers' actual classrooms.

**The insight:** Teachers don't need to become AI experts. They need to develop a mental model of how AI works, learn a transferable prompting framework, and build the judgment to decide when AI helps and when it doesn't — all within the context of their own teaching practice.

**The intervention:** A 7-week AI-guided course (~20 minutes per week, ~2 hours total) that meets teachers where they are — personalized to their subject, grade level, experience, and goals. Each week produces a concrete artifact. The course builds capacity, not dependency.

**The mechanism:**
1. Personalized onboarding captures teaching context
2. Diagnostic assessment (SOLO taxonomy) determines starting level
3. Adaptive scaffolding matches each teacher's readiness
4. Artifact construction ensures applied learning
5. External testing validates transferable skills
6. Reflection consolidates metacognitive growth

**The outcome:** Teachers who complete the course can independently craft effective AI prompts, critically evaluate AI outputs, make informed decisions about classroom AI use, and articulate a personal AI policy — skills that directly transfer to student preparation.

## Scalability Path

**Current state:** Post-MVP, functional product, small pilot user base.

**Phase 1 — Validated Pilot (Months 1-6):**
- 50-100 teachers across 3-5 NYC schools
- Research partnership for rigorous outcome measurement
- Iterate curriculum based on completion data and artifact quality
- Cost: ~$15-25 per teacher (API costs + hosting)

**Phase 2 — District Partnerships (Months 6-18):**
- Partner with 2-3 NYC districts for PD credit integration
- Add admin dashboard for district-level reporting
- Cohort model (teachers progress together with facilitated check-ins)
- Target: 500-1,000 teachers

**Phase 3 — National Scaling (Months 18-36):**
- Self-serve enrollment for individual teachers (freemium)
- District licensing model for school-wide adoption
- Advanced module library (subject-specific, role-specific)
- Target: 10,000+ teachers

**Unit economics at scale:**
- Marginal cost per teacher: ~$8-15 (API calls + hosting)
- No per-teacher content creation cost (curriculum is reusable)
- Revenue potential: $50-200/teacher (individual) or $5,000-20,000/district (site license)

## Sustainability Plan

### Path to Financial Sustainability

**Year 1 (Grant-funded):**
- Pilot program development and research validation
- Revenue: $0 (free pilot for participating schools)
- Funding need: $150K-300K

**Year 2 (Hybrid):**
- District licensing revenue begins
- Individual teacher freemium tier launches
- Revenue: $50K-150K
- Funding need: $100K-200K (gap funding)

**Year 3+ (Self-sustaining):**
- 50+ district partnerships
- Advanced module library (paid)
- Potential certification partnership (PD credits)
- Revenue: $300K-1M+
- Funding need: $0 (profitable)

### Revenue Model
1. **District licensing**: Annual per-teacher fee for school/district-wide access with admin analytics
2. **Individual subscriptions**: Freemium (Weeks 0-1 free) with paid access to full course
3. **Advanced modules**: Subject-specific or role-specific add-on modules
4. **Certification partnerships**: Co-branded PD credit programs with state education agencies

### Why This Is Sustainable
- **Near-zero marginal content cost**: The curriculum doesn't change per teacher — personalization is handled by the AI
- **Low per-teacher API cost**: ~$8-15 per teacher for 7 weeks of AI interactions
- **High perceived value**: Comparable to $500-2,000 multi-day PD workshops, delivered at a fraction of the cost
- **Natural expansion**: Each module is curriculum, not code — new topics require content, not engineering
