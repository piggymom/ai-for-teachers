### Notes on Tier 1

**Spencer Foundation** is the most accessible immediately. They fund education research from individuals — no 501(c)(3) needed. Frame Skippy as a research project studying the impact of AI-guided PD on teacher AI literacy. $50K funds a rigorous 25-teacher micro-pilot.

**NSF (DRK-12 / ITEST)** is the largest funding opportunity but requires a university partner as PI. Reach out to education schools with AI/technology programs. You'd be co-PI or senior personnel delivering the intervention; the university handles the grant submission.

**ISTE** may be more valuable as a partner than a funder. They've distributed Google.org funds for teacher AI training. Getting ISTE endorsement or co-branding could open doors to their funding network and credibility.

**Google.org** doesn't maintain a rolling application — they issue periodic challenges. Monitor google.org/blog for announcements. The AI Opportunity Fund ($75M) has partnered with organizations like ISTE to distribute training grants.

## The 501(c)(3) Question

### Most funding requires it. Here are your options:

**Option 1: Fiscal Sponsorship (Fastest)**
Organizations like New Venture Fund, Tides Foundation, or smaller local sponsors will hold 501(c)(3) status for you for 5-10% of grant funds. This unlocks most foundation grants within weeks.

**Option 2: University Partnership (Best for NSF)**
A university PI submits the grant; you are co-PI or senior personnel. The university's 501(c)(3) status and infrastructure covers the requirement. This is the only path for NSF funding.

**Option 3: Apply Where You Can Without It**
Spencer Foundation, Camelback Ventures, and some smaller programs accept individual applicants. Start here to build evidence and credibility.

**Option 4: Form Your Own 501(c)(3) (Longest)**
Takes 3-6 months. Only worth it if you're committed to this as a long-term nonprofit venture. Consider after pilot results are in.
