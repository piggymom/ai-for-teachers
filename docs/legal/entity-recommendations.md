## Context

## Options

### Option 1: Sole Proprietorship (Current State)

**What it is:** Operating as an individual without a separate legal entity.

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| No setup cost or paperwork | **No liability protection** — personal assets at risk |
| Simplest tax filing (Schedule C) | Difficult to sign institutional contracts (school DPAs) |
| No ongoing compliance requirements | Less credible to school districts |
| Immediate — already in this state | Cannot bring on co-founders/investors easily |
| | Personal liability for data breaches |

**Recommendation:** Not suitable for handling teacher data or signing district agreements. Move to at least an LLC before the pilot.

---

### Option 2: LLC (Limited Liability Company)

**What it is:** A flexible business entity that separates personal and business liability.

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| **Liability protection** — personal assets shielded | Formation cost (~$200–$800 depending on state) |
| Can sign contracts as an entity | Annual filing requirements and fees |
| Can open business bank account | Need to maintain separation of finances |
| Flexible taxation (pass-through or elect S-Corp) | Self-employment taxes on profit |
| Simple to form and maintain | State-specific rules vary |
| Can add members later | |
| Credible to districts and institutions | |

**Formation details:**
- **Recommended state:** New York (if primarily serving NYC teachers) or Delaware (for flexibility)
- **NY LLC cost:** ~$200 filing fee + $50 biennial statement + publication requirement (~$300–$1,500 in NY)
- **Delaware LLC cost:** ~$90 filing fee + $300/year franchise tax + registered agent (~$100/year)
- **Timeline:** 1–4 weeks

**Tax implications:**
- Default: pass-through taxation (profits reported on personal return)
- Can elect S-Corp taxation if profitable (saves self-employment tax above ~$50K profit)

**Recommendation:** **Best option for the pilot stage.** Provides liability protection with minimal overhead. Can always convert to nonprofit later if that path makes sense.

### Option 3: Nonprofit (501(c)(3))

**What it is:** A tax-exempt organization operated for charitable, educational, or scientific purposes.

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| **Tax-exempt** — no federal income tax | Complex formation (articles, bylaws, board) |
| **Grant-eligible** — can apply for education grants | IRS 501(c)(3) application ($275–$600 fee, 3–12 months) |
| Donors get tax deductions | **Requires a board of directors** (minimum 3 in most states) |
| Strong credibility with schools and districts | Restrictions on activities and compensation |
| Mission-aligned framing | Cannot distribute profits to founders |
| May qualify for free/discounted SaaS tools | More complex accounting and reporting (Form 990) |
| | Cannot easily convert to for-profit later |
| | Founder cannot "own" the organization |

**Formation details:**
- **Step 1:** Form nonprofit corporation in state (~$75–$200)
- **Step 2:** Apply for IRS 501(c)(3) status (Form 1023 or 1023-EZ)
- **Step 3:** Apply for state tax exemption
- **Timeline:** 3–12 months for full tax-exempt status
- **Ongoing:** Annual Form 990, board meetings, compliance

**Best for:** If the long-term vision is grant-funded, mission-driven, and Asher is comfortable not "owning" the organization.

---

### Option 4: B-Corp / Benefit Corporation

**What it is:** A for-profit corporation with a stated social mission, legally required to consider stakeholder impact.

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Mission-driven with for-profit flexibility | More complex than LLC |
| Can accept investment | Higher formation and compliance costs |
| Stakeholder protection in governance | B-Corp certification is separate from legal status |
| Growing recognition in education space | May be overkill for current stage |
| Can generate profit and pay founders | Less grant-eligible than nonprofit |

**Recommendation:** Consider later if AI for Teachers grows and seeks impact investment. Not necessary for pilot stage.

## Recommendation

### For the Pilot: Form an LLC

**Why:**
1. **Liability protection before handling real teacher data** — this is the minimum responsible step
2. **Fast to set up** — can be done in 1–2 weeks, well before pilot launch
3. **Low cost and maintenance** — ~$200–$800 to form, minimal ongoing requirements
4. **Sufficient for district contracts** — schools can sign DPAs with an LLC
5. **Flexible** — can convert to nonprofit later, add members, or restructure as needed
6. **Lets you focus on the product** — minimal administrative distraction

### Steps to Form LLC

1. **Choose state of formation** (NY recommended if serving NYC schools)
2. **Choose a name** (e.g., "AI for Teachers LLC" — check availability)
3. **File Articles of Organization** with state
4. **Complete NY publication requirement** (if NY LLC — publish in two newspapers for 6 weeks)
5. **Get an EIN** from the IRS (free, instant online)
6. **Open a business bank account** (keep finances separate)
7. **Draft an Operating Agreement** (even as single-member — establishes legitimacy)
8. **Register for any needed state/local taxes**

### Later Decision: Nonprofit Conversion

After the pilot, if the evidence supports it, consider converting to or forming a parallel nonprofit:

**Indicators that nonprofit makes sense:**
- Pursuing education grants (federal, state, foundation)
- Schools prefer or require contracting with nonprofits
- Revenue model is primarily grant/donation funded
- Mission alignment is more important than founder ownership

**Indicators that LLC is better long-term:**
- Planning to charge subscription fees as primary revenue
- Want to retain ownership and flexibility
- May seek investment or acquisition
- Want simpler governance
