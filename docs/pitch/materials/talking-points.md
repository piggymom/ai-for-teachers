# Talking Points — AI for Teachers

## The 30-Second Version

"I built an AI tutor that teaches teachers to use AI. It's a 7-week personalized course — 20 minutes a week — and every session produces a classroom-ready artifact. The learning science adapts to each teacher in real time using SOLO taxonomy. And here's the key: we deliberately send teachers to practice in ChatGPT and Gemini, because we're building capacity, not dependency. The whole course costs about $5 per teacher to deliver. Traditional PD costs $50-200 per session."

---

## The 2-Minute Version

"There are 3.7 million teachers in America, and the Department of Labor just told every district they need AI literacy professional development. But the PD market is broken — $50-200 per teacher for one-shot workshops that everyone forgets by Monday.

Meanwhile, the first wave of AI tools for teachers has a fundamental problem: they generate content *for* teachers instead of building teacher capability. I call it the Ozempic problem — the tool works while it's on, but when you remove it, the capability goes with it.

I built Skippy — an AI tutor that teaches teachers to use AI by having them *build things*, not watch slides. It's a 7-week course. Each session is 20 minutes. Each session produces one concrete artifact the teacher uses in their classroom. The system diagnoses each teacher's understanding level in real time using SOLO taxonomy and adapts the instruction automatically.

The most important design decision: we send teachers to practice their skills in ChatGPT and Gemini. We deliberately build independence, not lock-in. When the course is over, the skills remain.

The cost to deliver is about $5 per teacher for the full course. I've already built and deployed the product — it's live, not a prototype. My previous tool, TimeSaveAI, has over 100 weekly users. And I delivered AI literacy PD at New Visions for Public Schools last month.

I'm raising $500K to run a 200-teacher pilot in NYC and scale the product."

## Common Q&A Responses

### "Why should I believe this works?"
"Two things. First, the learning science is established — SOLO taxonomy has 40 years of validation. We're applying a proven diagnostic framework to a new domain. Second, TimeSaveAI already has 100+ weekly users using the same design philosophy. The approach works. We're raising for the formal pilot to prove it at scale."

### "What happens after 7 weeks?"
"Teachers have a portfolio of 7 artifacts they built themselves and skills that transfer to any AI tool. For those who want more, we're building advanced modules — special education, ELL, STEM-specific, subject-specific. Each module is content, not code — low marginal cost to develop."

### "Why would a district pay for this?"
"Three reasons. One: it's 10-40x cheaper than traditional PD. Two: it comes with completion data and learning progression metrics — evidence for their board. Three: it builds skills their teachers keep even if they stop paying us. That's a value proposition no other AI PD product offers."

### "Aren't you just a ChatGPT wrapper?"
"There are 4,134 lines of pedagogical prompt content across 7 week-specific modules. A real-time SOLO taxonomy classifier. A conversation ledger tracking phase, level, and skill progression. A 6-layer personalization engine. That's like calling a university a 'building wrapper.' The infrastructure is AI. The product is curriculum."

### "Can teachers just use ChatGPT directly?"
"They can. And they should — that's literally what we teach them to do. The question is whether they'll use it *well*. ChatGPT has no scaffolding, no progression, no diagnostic, and no accountability. Most teachers who try it once get mediocre results and give up. We provide the structured learning path that turns dabbling into capability."

### "What about equity?"
"$5 per teacher. Self-paced. No travel required. No substitute teacher needed. Works on any device with a browser. Designed for teachers who haven't started yet — our primary persona is 'curious but overwhelmed.' The cost structure doesn't just aspire to equity — it makes it structurally possible."

### "Why you?"
"I've spent 15 years in classrooms — in the U.S., Australia, and Finland. I'm not a tech founder who discovered education. I'm a teacher who learned to build. I built TimeSaveAI because I saw teachers wasting hours on observation feedback. I built Skippy because I saw teachers anxious about AI with no good way to learn. Everything I build starts from the same place: what do teachers actually need, and what's the most respectful way to deliver it?"

## Stories to Tell

### The New Visions Moment
"Last month I delivered AI literacy PD at New Visions for Public Schools — one of NYC's largest education organizations. I put eight AI use cases on a Miro board and asked teachers to drag them along a scale from 'Totally Fine' to 'Crosses a Line.' The spread was enormous. Teachers in the same building had fundamentally different intuitions about where the line is. That's when it hit me: AI literacy isn't about skills alone. It's about values. And every teacher needs a safe space to figure out where their line sits."

### The External Testing Loop
"The most counterintuitive decision I made was sending teachers away from my product. In Week 3, Skippy says: 'This prompt looks ready. Go try it in ChatGPT. Come back and tell me what happened.' Every product manager instinct says: keep the user inside. Maximize engagement. But if I'm serious about building capacity, the teacher has to practice in the wild. If they can only write good prompts inside Skippy, I haven't taught them anything."

### The Ozempic Insight
"I was reading about Ozempic — how patients lose weight while they're on it, but when they stop, the weight comes back. Because the drug suppresses the hunger that drives behavior change. And I thought: that's exactly what we're doing with AI in education. The tools suppress the struggle of learning to work with AI. But the struggle is where the learning happens. That's when I decided: Skippy would be a coach, not a crutch."
