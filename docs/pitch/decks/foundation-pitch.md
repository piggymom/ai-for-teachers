## Slide 1: The Dependency Problem

> "When students using AI tutoring tools had access removed, performance dropped 17%. Most AI-in-education tools create the same dependency for teachers."

We call this the Ozempic problem: suppress the struggle, lose the growth.

**What if we built AI professional development that did the opposite?**

---

## Slide 2: The Problem

**Teachers need AI literacy. Current approaches don't work.**

- The U.S. Department of Labor released its first AI Literacy Framework in February 2026 — districts must now address teacher AI competency
- Traditional PD costs **$50-200 per teacher per session** and teachers overwhelmingly describe it as ineffective
- First-generation AI tools (MagicSchool, Khanmigo) generate content *for* teachers — building dependency, not capability
- **82% of K-12 teachers report feeling unprepared to use AI effectively**

The gap: no structured, personalized, evidence-based program exists to build teacher AI *capacity* at scale.

## Slide 3: Our Approach — Capacity Over Dependency

**Skippy is an AI tutor that teaches teachers to use AI — by building, not watching.**

| Design Principle | What It Means |
|-----------------|---------------|
| **Artifact-first** | Every 20-minute session produces a concrete classroom tool (prompt template, lesson workflow, feedback rubric) |
| **Adaptive scaffolding** | SOLO taxonomy classifier diagnoses understanding in real time and adjusts instruction |
| **Tool-agnostic** | Teachers practice in ChatGPT and Gemini — skills transfer to any platform |
| **Personalized** | 6-layer prompt engine adapts to teacher's subject, grade, goals, and current understanding |

The external testing loop is deliberate: Skippy coaches the teacher, then sends them to practice independently. When the coaching ends, the capability remains.

## Slide 4: The 7-Week Curriculum

| Week | Focus | Artifact Produced | Learning Science |
|------|-------|-------------------|-----------------|
| 0 | Onboarding | Teaching profile | Personalization baseline |
| 1 | Understanding AI | AI mental model card | Pre-structural → Unistructural |
| 2 | Prompting Fundamentals | 4C prompt template | Framework acquisition |
| 3 | Lesson Planning | Planning workflow | Framework application |
| 4 | Feedback & Assessment | Feedback template | Framework transfer |
| 5 | Differentiation | Differentiation template | Complex application |
| 6 | Ethics & Integration | Personal AI policy | Extended abstract synthesis |

**Time commitment:** ~20 minutes per week. No scheduling required. No substitute needed.

**SOLO taxonomy progression:** The curriculum is designed to move teachers from pre-structural understanding ("I don't know what AI can do") to extended abstract ("I can teach others and evaluate AI tools critically").

## Slide 5: The Learning Science

**This is pedagogy, not prompt tips.**

**SOLO Taxonomy (Biggs & Collis, 1982)**
A real-time diagnostic classifier runs after every teacher response (zero-latency, async). It:
- Diagnoses the teacher's SOLO level with evidence
- Tracks conversation phase (Discover → Build → Refine → Reflect → Save → Bridge)
- Monitors skill component completion (4C framework: Context, Constraints, Command, Criteria)
- Generates adaptive guidance for the next turn

**Observable behavior change by level:**
- **Pre-structural:** Step-by-step walkthroughs, concrete analogies, celebrating small wins
- **Unistructural:** Introducing structure to existing intuitions
- **Multistructural:** Challenging mechanical application — "Which component is doing the work here?"
- **Relational+:** Peer mode. Edge cases. "Where does this framework break down?"

**4,134 lines of pedagogical prompt content** across 7 week-specific system prompts. This is curriculum IP, not a ChatGPT wrapper.

## Slide 6: Measurable Outcomes

**Every interaction generates learning data.**

| Metric | How It's Measured | Why It Matters |
|--------|------------------|---------------|
| SOLO level progression | Async classifier per exchange | Are teachers actually learning? |
| 4C component mastery | Sticky boolean tracking | Are prompting skills being acquired? |
| Artifact quality | LLM evaluation + human review | Are outputs classroom-ready? |
| External testing completion | Self-reported in conversation | Are teachers applying skills independently? |
| Course completion rate | Per-week progress tracking | Is the program retaining teachers? |
| Transfer to independent use | Post-course survey + follow-up | Does capability persist without Skippy? |

**The pilot will generate:** Pre/post SOLO assessments, artifact portfolio quality scores, independent AI use rates at 30/60/90 days post-completion, and qualitative interview data.

## Slide 9: Pilot Design

**Proposed: 10 NYC schools, 200 teachers, 14 weeks**

| Phase | Duration | Activities |
|-------|----------|-----------|
| **Setup** | 2 weeks | School recruitment, teacher consent, baseline SOLO assessment |
| **Intervention** | 7 weeks | Teachers complete the Skippy course at their own pace |
| **Follow-up** | 4 weeks | Post-course independent AI use tracking, interviews |
| **Analysis** | 1 week | Data analysis, preliminary findings report |

**Data collection:**
- Pre/post SOLO taxonomy assessment (validated instrument)
- Weekly artifact quality scoring (rubric + LLM evaluation)
- Conversation logs (engagement depth, external testing loop completion)
- Teacher interviews (qualitative, 20% sample)
- 30/60/90 day follow-up survey (independent AI use, skill transfer)

**IRB pathway:** Partnership with education research institution (Columbia Teachers College, NYU Steinhardt, or Hunter College School of Education)

## Slide 10: Sustainability

**This sustains beyond the grant.**

- **Unit economics:** ~$5/teacher (Claude API) against $25-50/teacher B2B pricing = 80-90% gross margin
- **Post-pilot revenue model:** District licensing for admin dashboard, cohort management, PD credit certification
- **Curriculum expansion:** Advanced modules (special ed, ELL, STEM) are content, not code — marginal development cost
- **Network effects:** Teacher testimonials and artifact sharing drive organic adoption
- **Open curriculum option:** Core 7-week course published under Creative Commons; premium features (adaptive scaffolding, analytics) sustain the organization
