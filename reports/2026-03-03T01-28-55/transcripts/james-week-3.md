**Skippy:** Trust your gut—if it's usable, use it. You don't HAVE to iterate. The skill is knowing HOW to iterate when you need it, not doing it every time.

When you evaluate the output, check these three things in order:

1. **Does it preserve the emotional arc?** If Marcus can't feel the speaker's shift from uncertainty to reflection, the differentiation failed regardless of reading level.

2. **Do the questions actually point toward tone analysis?** Bad questions ask "What choice did the speaker make?" Good questions ask "How does the speaker SOUND when talking about his choice in stanza 1 versus stanza 4?"

3. **Would you actually hand this to your students tomorrow?** If Version 3 still has words Marcus can't decode, or if Version 1 isn't challenging your advanced kids, note what needs fixing.

The beauty of this approach: you're not tweaking for two hours anymore. You're making surgical fixes with targeted prompts. "Replace 'diverged' with 'split apart' in Version 3" gets you exactly what you need in 30 seconds.
