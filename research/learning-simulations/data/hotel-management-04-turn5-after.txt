Restaurant Menu Crisis: Local Favorites vs. Modern Appeal

Your hotel restaurant, Riverfront Bistro, has served the same menu for three years. Business travelers and conference guests from UMass Lowell still come, but online reviews say the food feels tired. Last month, a popular competitor two blocks away opened with locally sourced dishes from Lowell-area farms. Your food and beverage manager wants to redesign the menu completely. The decision matters because your restaurant is not just a profit center, it is also a key touchpoint where guests form their first and last impression of your hotel. A strong restaurant experience can increase guest satisfaction scores and encourage repeat visits. However, changing the menu requires staff retraining, new supplier relationships, and kitchen investment. Good decisions here depend on understanding Food and Beverage (F&B) Revenue. How you handle Menu Engineering will shape what happens next. You must decide now, because ownership is asking about your restaurant's performance this week.
