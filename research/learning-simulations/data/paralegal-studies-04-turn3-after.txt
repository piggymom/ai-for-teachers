The Missing Court Precedent

You are organizing legal research files for a personal injury case involving a car accident on Bridge Street in Lowell. Your supervising attorney, Ms. Chen, asked you to find similar court cases to support your client's claim for medical expenses and lost wages. You searched the law library database and found three cases that seem helpful, but you are not completely sure if they are the strongest examples. The problem is that your research is not finished, but Ms. Chen needs your findings tomorrow morning before the judge conference call. You must decide how much time to spend finding better cases and how much research quality to report. If you rush and miss important cases, your client might lose money they deserve. How you handle Legal precedent will shape what happens next. As a lead paralegal, you need to think about Due diligence before deciding. If you spend too much time, you might miss the deadline and disappoint your attorney and client.
