Rush Mediation or Prepare Carefully?

It is Tuesday morning at Merrimack Valley Law Offices in downtown Lowell. You are the lead paralegal on a family law case. The case involves two parents fighting over custody of their child. The judge ordered mediation to happen this Friday, just three days away. Your attorney, Mr. Chen, is busy with three other court appearances this week. Your job is to prepare all the documents and facts that the mediator will need. You must organize witness statements, financial records, and the custody proposal into one clear package. If you prepare well, the mediation might succeed and save the family time and money. This situation is closely connected to Discovery and how you manage it. If you rush or skip important details, the mediation could fail and the case goes back to court, which costs everyone more money and stress.
